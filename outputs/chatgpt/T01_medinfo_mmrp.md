## Summary

In the RUBY trial, dostarlimab (JEMPERLI) in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel, followed by dostarlimab monotherapy, was evaluated in patients with primary advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer (EC). Prespecified exploratory analyses in the mismatch repair proficient/microsatellite stable (MMRp/MSS) subgroup showed numerical improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with placebo plus carboplatin and paclitaxel. However, the confidence intervals for both hazard ratios included 1, and these subgroup findings should be interpreted cautiously.

## Labeled indication

JEMPERLI is indicated in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel, followed by JEMPERLI as a single agent, for the treatment of adult patients with primary advanced or recurrent EC. This indication is not restricted by mismatch repair status.

## Efficacy in the MMRp/MSS subgroup

RUBY was a randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 494 patients with primary advanced or recurrent EC. Overall, 76% had MMRp/MSS tumors.

In the prespecified exploratory MMRp/MSS analyses (n = 372):

- OS: Median OS was 32.5 months (95% CI: 28.6, NR) with dostarlimab plus chemotherapy versus 28.2 months (95% CI: 21.9, 36.1) with placebo plus chemotherapy; hazard ratio (HR) 0.82 (95% CI: 0.62, 1.08).
- PFS: Median PFS was 9.8 months (95% CI: 9.0, 12.6) with dostarlimab plus chemotherapy (n = 185) versus 7.9 months (95% CI: 7.6, 9.8) with placebo plus chemotherapy (n = 187); HR 0.78 (95% CI: 0.60, 1.00).

These exploratory subgroup results do not establish a statistically significant OS or PFS benefit specifically in the MMRp/MSS population.

## Safety

In the overall RUBY safety population, serious adverse reactions occurred in 39% of patients receiving dostarlimab plus carboplatin and paclitaxel. Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 1.2%, permanent discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in 19%, and dosage interruptions occurred in 37%.

Common adverse reactions included peripheral neuropathy, fatigue, nausea, alopecia, rash, and diarrhea. Dostarlimab can also cause severe or fatal immune-mediated adverse reactions, including pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, and nephritis.

The supplied prescribing information does not report safety outcomes separately for the MMRp/MSS subgroup.
