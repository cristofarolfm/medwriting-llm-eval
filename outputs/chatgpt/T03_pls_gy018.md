## NRG-GY018 Clinical Trial: A Summary for Patients and Caregivers

The NRG-GY018 study tested whether adding a drug called pembrolizumab to standard chemotherapy (paclitaxel and carboplatin) could help people with advanced or returning endometrial cancer (cancer of the lining of the uterus).

The study included 813 patients. Some received pembrolizumab with chemotherapy, while others received chemotherapy with a placebo (a treatment with no active drug).

Patients were divided into two groups based on a feature of their cancer cells:

- dMMR: Cancer cells with problems repairing damaged DNA.
- pMMR: Cancer cells with a working DNA repair system.

### What did the study find?

Researchers measured how long patients lived without their cancer getting worse.

- dMMR group: Patients receiving chemotherapy alone had a median time of 7.6 months before their cancer worsened or they died. For those receiving pembrolizumab, the median had not yet been reached.
- pMMR group: The median time was 11.7 months with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy, compared with 8.7 months with chemotherapy alone.

### What about safety?

Serious side effects were reported more often in patients receiving pembrolizumab with chemotherapy in both groups.

### What is not yet known?

The available results do not show whether adding pembrolizumab helps patients live longer overall. More information is needed to understand its long-term benefits and risks.

Source: ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT03914612 (NRG-GY018), posted study results.&#x20;
