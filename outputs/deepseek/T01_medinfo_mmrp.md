**Summary**  
You asked whether adding dostarlimab to carboplatin and paclitaxel helps patients with mismatch repair proficient (MMRp)/microsatellite stable (MSS) endometrial cancer. The JEMPERLI prescribing information reports pre-specified exploratory analyses from RUBY in MMRp/MSS EC (n = 372): OS hazard ratio (HR) 0.82 (95% CI: 0.62, 1.08) and PFS HR 0.78 (95% CI: 0.60, 1.00). These are exploratory subgroup analyses, and the confidence intervals include or touch 1.00, so they should be interpreted cautiously. All data below are from jemperli_label.md.

**Labeled indication**  
JEMPERLI, in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel, followed by JEMPERLI as a single agent, is indicated for adult patients with primary advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer. This combination indication does not specify MMR status. Separate single-agent indications are limited to dMMR recurrent or advanced EC or dMMR recurrent or advanced solid tumors. Source: jemperli_label.md.

**Efficacy in the MMRp/MSS subgroup**  
In RUBY, 76% of 494 patients had MMRp/MSS tumors. In the MMRp/MSS subgroup (n = 372), median OS was 32.5 (95% CI: 28.6, NR) months with JEMPERLI plus carboplatin and paclitaxel versus 28.2 (95% CI: 21.9, 36.1) months with placebo plus carboplatin and paclitaxel; OS HR was 0.82 (95% CI: 0.62, 1.08). Median PFS was 9.8 (95% CI: 9.0, 12.6) months with JEMPERLI combination (n = 185) versus 7.9 (95% CI: 7.6, 9.8) months with placebo combination (n = 187); PFS HR was 0.78 (95% CI: 0.60, 1.00). Source: jemperli_label.md.

**Safety**  
Safety was evaluated in 241 patients with primary advanced or recurrent EC receiving JEMPERLI with carboplatin and paclitaxel in RUBY; these data are not MMRp/MSS-specific. Serious adverse reactions occurred in 39%; common serious reactions were sepsis, including urosepsis (3.7%), and pulmonary embolism (3.3%). Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 1.2%, including septic shock (0.8%) and myelosuppression (0.4%). Permanent discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in 46 patients (19%); dosage interruptions occurred in 37%. The most common adverse reactions (≥20%), including laboratory abnormalities, were decreased hemoglobin, increased creatinine, peripheral neuropathy, decreased white blood cell count, fatigue, nausea, alopecia, decreased platelets, increased glucose, decreased lymphocytes, decreased magnesium, decreased neutrophils, increased AST, arthralgia, rash, constipation, diarrhea, increased ALT, decreased potassium, decreased albumin, decreased sodium, increased alkaline phosphatase, abdominal pain, dyspnea, decreased appetite, increased amylase, decreased phosphate, urinary tract infection, and vomiting. Source: jemperli_label.md.
